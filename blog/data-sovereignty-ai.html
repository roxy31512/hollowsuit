Can a Middle Power Buy AI Sovereignty? Canada's $1.6B Bet on Cohere

November 2026

Canada committed to invest up to $1.6B into Cohere AI the same day Alphabet lost ~$480B in a day over concerns about AI spending. Ottawa will be a customer, policymaker and shareholder of Cohere.

Canada is betting on a sovereign approach to AI with a smaller, more targeted bet on Cohere, headquartered in Toronto.

Middle powers, including Canada, have been taking greater steps to become sovereign from foreign AI models, including American and Chinese. Carney framed the 29 October 2026 investment into Cohere as the ability to build, adopt, and govern AI on Canada's own terms and have greater control over where sensitive data is stored, how it's used, and who can access it. Middle powers don't need to enter the AGI race and even Alphabet is being punished by investors over the cash cost of AI spending. Alphabet alone has guided to roughly $195B to $205B of capex this year, and middle powers are unlikely to fund something similar.

Yet, it remains unclear how the Canadian government will address the perception and conflict-of-interest of a government with a financial interest in a vendor. This investment doesn't automatically make Cohere the preferred choice for the government and Canadian businesses, as the Buy Canadian policy treats foreign firms with subsidiaries in Canada the same as Canadian-headquartered companies.

One other interesting element is that this is of course taxpayer money. For $1.6B, Canadians are promised 1,000 jobs, $1.7B compute, Canadian incorporation and headquarters. It's unclear if the jobs will go to Canadians or some will also go to foreigners. It's difficult to judge what may happen if Cohere doesn't turn profit on the investment because the government hasn't disclosed yet share class, governance rights, what triggers each tranche, and whether there's an exit plan. Cohere's valuation has risen from $7B USD last round to $35.7B CAD this round, a three-fold conversion in about a year in between. So Ottawa is buying in after a steep rise. Whether taxpayers get their money back depends on the valuation holding.
